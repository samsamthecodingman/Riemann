# 03 — Absorbing and displaying information for adults with ADHD

Researcher: claude-adhd-researcher. Primary user in view: Sam, an adult engineering student with ADHD, using Riemann's zoom-dial abstraction tree.

## TL;DR

- Adult ADHD's best-replicated cognitive markers are **working-memory deficits** (moderate effect) and **intra-individual response-time variability** (moderate-to-large, and distinct from simple slowness) — the practical translation is: don't design for a *constant* attention budget, design for one that spikes and craters unpredictably within a single session.
- **Delay aversion / reward-sensitivity** (dual-pathway model) means the *subjective cost of waiting* is itself aversive, independent of executive function — so anything that defers gratification (a 20-minute read before any payoff) taxes ADHD users more than the word count alone suggests.
- Dodson's "interest-based nervous system" (PINCH: passion, interest, novelty, challenge/competition) is **practitioner theory, not peer-reviewed construct** — treat it as a strong design heuristic, not an established mechanism.
- **Bimodal reading (text + synced audio) has real peer-reviewed support**, including for weaker/less-skilled readers and vocabulary acquisition — this is one of the more actionable, well-evidenced levers available.
- **Bionic reading has no credible evidence for ADHD** — an EEG study found no added benefit, and a 2022 controlled study found no comprehension/speed gain over normal text. Popular but unsupported.
- **White noise helps some ADHD readers and hurts others**: inattentive-predominant profiles benefit (stochastic-resonance / moderate-brain-arousal account), hyperactive/impulsive-predominant profiles can get *worse* — a uniform "add background noise" feature is a coin flip without knowing the subtype/state.
- **Fidgeting/movement while working measurably improves task performance** in both children and adults with ADHD, especially as task duration/attention wanes — this argues for *not* penalizing or eliminating physical restlessness in any recommended "focus session" pattern.
- Chunking and short blocks/white space are core, well-established recommendations in accessibility literature (W3C COGA) — but the *general* "bullet points reduce cognitive load" research is mostly UX-blog-tier, not peer-reviewed cognitive science; treat separately from COGA's disability-specific guidance.
- Emotionally, **rejection-sensitive dysphoria** and the **Wall of Awful** (practitioner concepts, not clinical diagnoses) describe a shame/avoidance spiral that specifically attaches to *starting* — this is arguably more load-bearing for a "large chunk of content" product than any purely cognitive finding, because the abstraction tree's job is to lower the activation energy of the first click.
- **Body doubling** is hugely popular in ADHD communities but has almost no controlled evidence — one 12-person VR pilot is the closest thing to data.
- Medication state is a live confound for any adaptive system: onset/offset windows (30–45 min onset, 3–14 hr duration depending on formulation) mean the *same user* has meaningfully different working memory and impulse control at 9am vs 4pm, independent of anything Riemann does.

## Findings

### Cognitive profile

**F1. Working-memory deficits persist into adulthood (moderate effect size).**
A meta-analysis of 38 studies found moderate-magnitude between-group effects for both phonological and visuospatial working memory in adults with ADHD vs. controls, with several task-moderating variables explaining variability across studies.
Evidence: MODERATE (single meta-analysis, but well-cited and methodologically explicit about moderators).
Source: [Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and working memory in adults: a meta-analytic review — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23688211/)

**F2. Intra-individual reaction-time variability (RTV) is a more distinctive marker of ADHD than mean slowness, and it shrinks somewhat but persists into adulthood.**
A meta-analysis of 319 studies found children/adolescents with ADHD show large RTV elevation vs. typically developing peers (Hedges' g ≈ 0.76), adults show a smaller but still real effect (g ≈ 0.46). Critically, once RTV is accounted for, ADHD groups do *not* show slower mean processing speed — the variability, not the average, is the signature. RTV was reduced by stimulant medication (g ≈ -0.74) but not by non-stimulant or psychosocial interventions.
Evidence: STRONG (large meta-analysis; effect replicated across studies and age groups).
Source: [Reaction time variability in ADHD: a meta-analytic review of 319 studies — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23872284/) (Kofler et al., 2013)

**F3. Delay aversion is a distinct pathway from executive dysfunction (dual-pathway model).**
The dual-pathway model proposes ADHD arises via two largely independent routes: executive/cognitive dysregulation (inhibition, working memory) and motivational dysregulation (delay aversion, altered reward-delay signaling via dopaminergic frontoventral-striatal circuits, especially nucleus accumbens). These two pathways can operate independently or interact, and they help explain within-diagnosis heterogeneity.
Evidence: MODERATE-STRONG (an influential, widely-cited theoretical model with converging behavioral and some neuroimaging support, though "dual pathway" is a framework more than a single confirmed mechanism).
Sources: [The dual pathway model of AD/HD — ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763403001052), [Delay Aversion in ADHD: broader phenotype — ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393208003837)

**F4. "Interest-based nervous system" (Dodson) is clinician-derived pop-clinical theory, not a peer-reviewed construct.**
Dodson (an adult-ADHD psychiatrist) popularized the claim that ADHD attention is governed by interest, novelty, challenge/competition, and urgency (PINCH/ICNU) rather than importance or consequence. This is widely repeated in ADHD media (ADDitude, CHADD-adjacent outlets) but I found no peer-reviewed operationalization or controlled test of the construct itself — it is consistent with, but not identical to, the reward-sensitivity and delay-aversion literature above.
Evidence: WEAK/PRELIMINARY — label explicitly as practitioner theory, useful as a design heuristic but not to be cited as established science.
Sources: [Is ADHD an Interest Based Nervous System? — Dr. William Dodson](https://adhdessentials.podbean.com/e/is-adhd-an-interest-based-nervous-system-with-dr-william-dodson/), [neurodivergentinsights.com summary](https://neurodivergentinsights.com/interest-based-nervous-system/)

**F5. Default Mode Network (DMN) regulation is atypical in ADHD but its link to mind-wandering is more nuanced than the popular "DMN doesn't switch off" story.**
A meta-analysis of 20 resting-state studies (944 ADHD vs. 1,121 controls) found *reduced* connectivity in the core DMN (posterior cingulate-centered) but *elevated* connectivity in the dorsomedial prefrontal subsystem. A separate adolescent study found DMN connectivity was associated with delay aversion and temporal discounting — but **not** directly with mind-wandering, complicating the simple "DMN intrusion causes ADHD mind-wandering" narrative.
Evidence: MODERATE (meta-analytic connectivity findings are reasonably solid; the causal link to subjective mind-wandering is contested/nuanced).
Sources: [DMN connectivity and ADHD in adolescence — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35032471), summarized via search of Frontiers/ScienceDirect coverage of the same study.

**F6. Time blindness and hyperfocus are two faces of the same time-perception deficit, championed by Barkley as central to ADHD.**
Russell Barkley frames ADHD as fundamentally a disorder of time perception and self-regulation rather than attention per se. Hyperfocus (losing track of time inside an absorbing task) and time blindness (chronic misjudgment of duration/deadlines) are described as the same underlying deficit expressed in opposite directions.
Evidence: MODERATE — this is a well-known clinical framework (Barkley) with converging behavioral evidence on time estimation, but much of the specific "time blindness" literature is clinical-practice writing rather than tightly controlled experiments; treat the *label* as practitioner-coined even though the underlying deficit (poor time estimation/prospective memory) is reasonably well studied.
Sources: [ADDA — ADHD Time Blindness](https://add.org/adhd-time-blindness/), [Psychology Today — How ADHD Leads to Time Blindness](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/disability-is-diversity/202311/how-adhd-leads-to-time-blindness)

### Reading, comprehension, and modality

**F7. Bimodal (simultaneous text + audio) presentation measurably helps weaker readers, with some caveats from cognitive-load theory.**
Controlled studies (Montali & Lewandowski 1996; more recent eye-tracking work by Valentini et al. and Rogowsky et al. 2016) show that less-skilled readers' comprehension in the bimodal condition matches average readers' comprehension in the visual-only condition, and bimodal presentation aids vocabulary acquisition. However, cognitive-load-theory researchers note that redundant bimodal input can also add extraneous processing load in some configurations (the "redundancy effect"), and a 2026 review explicitly flags gaps in the theoretical account of *why* reading-while-listening helps.
Evidence: MODERATE — real controlled/eye-tracking evidence for a benefit in weaker readers and vocabulary learning; genuinely mixed/contested for skilled readers and for comprehension broadly (not a clean STRONG).
Sources: [Rogowsky, Calhoun & Tallal 2016 — Does Modality Matter?](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244016669550), [Bimodal reading: benefits of a talking computer — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8732888/), [Online Processing Shows Advantages of Bimodal Listening-While-Reading — Reading Research Quarterly](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/rrq.522)

**F8. Bionic reading has no supporting evidence for ADHD readers and may have none at all.**
An EEG-based Utrecht University thesis study found bionic font offered no measurable benefit to ADHD readers. A separate 2022 University of Alabama controlled study found no significant improvement in reading speed or comprehension for bionic vs. normal text in general readers. No rigorous peer-reviewed study specifically targeting ADHD populations with a clear positive result was found.
Evidence: MYTH/DEBUNKED for the "speed reading" and "ADHD-specific benefit" claims specifically — treat popular enthusiasm for bionic reading as unsupported.
Sources: [Beyond the Hype: Bionic Reading EEG study — Utrecht University thesis repository](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/47156), [Can Bionic Reading make you a speed reader? Not so fast — The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/can-bionic-reading-make-you-a-speed-reader-not-so-fast-183905)

**F9. White noise/pink noise helps ADHD cognitive performance on average, via a "moderate brain arousal" / stochastic-resonance account — but the benefit is subtype-dependent and can reverse.**
The original Söderlund et al. 2007 study proposed stochastic resonance: noise boosts a weak neural signal, aiding ADHD performance on memory tasks; this was partially replicated in 2010, and a pilot study found white noise improved performance in both medicated and unmedicated ADHD children by a margin larger than the medication effect on some tasks. Critically, a more recent (2024) study found **inattentive-predominant** profiles benefit from noise, while **hyperactive/impulsive-predominant** profiles perform *worse* under the same noise — roughly a third of participants were harmed, not helped. A separate 2024 ScienceDirect paper explicitly challenges whether "stochastic resonance" is even the right mechanism for pink noise's benefits, suggesting the arousal-based account needs revision.
Evidence: MODERATE for a population-level noise benefit; the subtype-dependent reversal finding is newer/single-study and should be treated as MODERATE-WEAK pending replication. The mechanism (true stochastic resonance vs. general arousal) is actively contested.
Sources: [Söderlund et al. — Listen to the noise — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17683456/), [Sensory white noise in clinical ADHD: who benefits, who performs worse — PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11585357/), [Stochastic resonance not required for pink noise benefits — ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393224001763)

**F10. Fidgeting/movement improves sustained attention and task performance in ADHD, in both children and adults, and the benefit grows as time-on-task increases.**
Recent work (covered via UC Davis and CHADD summaries of peer-reviewed studies) finds adults with ADHD perform better on cognitive tasks when allowed to fidget, and unlike earlier assumptions that fidgeting is purely disruptive, it appears to function as a self-regulatory mechanism for arousal/alertness — the fidgeting *increases* as attention wanes over a task, consistent with it compensating for flagging arousal rather than merely reflecting boredom.
Evidence: MODERATE (multiple converging peer-reviewed studies cited by CHADD/UC Davis, consistent direction across ages, but I did not open the underlying primary papers directly — the summaries are from reputable secondary sources).
Sources: [UC Davis Health — Does fidgeting help people with ADHD focus?](https://health.ucdavis.edu/news/headlines/does-fidgeting-help-people-with-adhd-focus-/2024/10), [CHADD — How Does Fidgeting Enhance Focus](https://chadd.org/attention-article/how-does-fidgeting-enhance-focus-for-individuals-with-adhd/)

### Display/format interventions

**F11. W3C COGA's "Making Content Usable" guide is the most authoritative, standards-level source for chunking/structure/distraction-reduction, and it's disability-general (not ADHD-specific) but directly applicable.**
The living W3C draft (not yet a full Recommendation) organizes guidance into ~8 objectives with concrete patterns: use short chunks of content with generous white space; use plain, short sentences; provide clear headings/boundaries/regions and consistent, familiar navigation; let users turn off distractions; minimize memory demands (e.g., don't force re-entry of information, allow autofill); provide help accessible from anywhere in a task; support personalization. It also stresses testing with real users with cognitive disabilities.
Evidence: MODERATE-STRONG as *design guidance consensus* — it's not itself an experimental result, but it's a maintained, multi-stakeholder synthesis of accessibility research and is the closest thing to an authoritative standard in this space.
Source: [W3C COGA — Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](https://w3c.github.io/coga/content-usable/)

**F12. General "bullet points reduce cognitive load" claims are mostly marketing/UX-blog assertions, not peer-reviewed findings — distinguish from COGA's disability-specific guidance (F11).**
Searches for chunking/bullet-point research turned up almost entirely SEO/UX-industry content (Yoast, eLearning Industry, Pearson marketing pages) restating cognitive load theory in general terms, without citing controlled comprehension studies specific to bullets-vs-prose. Cognitive Load Theory itself (Sweller) is well established, but its specific application to "bullet points improve comprehension" is an inference, not a tested claim I could verify with a primary source.
Evidence: WEAK/PRELIMINARY for the bullet-specific claim; the underlying Cognitive Load Theory is STRONG but that's a different, broader claim already covered by the fleet's baseline research.

**F13. Gamification/immediate feedback shows real RCT-level benefit for ADHD attention and academic performance, at least in children.**
An 8-week RCT of a gamified educational application in children with ADHD found greater improvement than controls in visual/auditory reaction time, sustained attention, and reading/writing/math scores.
Evidence: MODERATE (single RCT, pediatric population — extrapolation to adult engineering-student use of a summarization tool is not directly tested, but the general "immediate feedback + game framing aids ADHD engagement" direction is consistent with dopamine/reward-sensitivity theory in F3).
Source: [Effectiveness of a gamified educational application on attention and academic performance in children with ADHD: 8-week RCT — Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1668260/epub)

### Digital tools & HCI

**F14. HCI venues (CHI, ASSETS, arXiv preprints) are actively producing ADHD-specific interaction research, but most is on self/co-regulation, video-watching customization, and body-doubling in VR — not on text abstraction/summarization UIs specifically.**
Notable recent work: *FocusView* (customizing informational video-watching for ADHD viewers), *You Are Not Alone* (VR body doubling for ADHD), *Orchestrating Attention* (neurodivergent learning states), and a Springer chapter on ADHD and knowledge work with AI. This suggests the "text-heavy content → digestible interactive form" problem Riemann is tackling is comparatively under-studied in ADHD HCI relative to video and co-presence tools — a gap worth flagging (see Gaps).
Evidence: N/A (this is a literature-coverage observation, not a claim to rate).
Sources: [FocusView — arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01248), [You Are Not Alone: VR Body Doubling — arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153), [ADHD and Knowledge Work: Exploring Strategies, Challenges and Opportunities for AI — Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-61688-4_10)

**F15. Real-world ADHD apps succeed by making time/tasks visually tangible and by separating "when" from "how to start."**
Tiimo (2025 Apple App of the Year) replaces text to-do lists with color-coded, icon-based visual timelines and countdown timers — addressing time blindness (F6) directly by making duration visible. Goblin.tools uses AI to break any single task into micro-steps, addressing task-initiation/Wall-of-Awful friction (F18) rather than scheduling. Reviewers consistently note Tiimo is strong on *when* but weak on *how to start* if the task itself feels overwhelming — implying these are two separable interventions, not one.
Evidence: WEAK/PRELIMINARY as evidence (these are product reviews and marketing-adjacent commentary, not controlled studies) but useful as validated-in-the-market design patterns.
Sources: [Tiimo App Review 2026](https://aiinsightsnews.net/tiimo/), [Honest Goblin.tools review 2026](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/goblin-tools-review)

### Emotional factors

**F16. Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) is a widely used clinical-practice term for an intense shame/rejection reaction, linked speculatively to dopamine deficits, and it drives avoidance-based perfectionism and task paralysis.**
RSD is described (Dodson again, plus a body of clinician/patient writing) as sudden emotional flooding triggered by perceived criticism or a sense of falling short, which manifests behaviorally as procrastination, inability to submit "imperfect" work, and avoidance of tasks where failure is possible. The dopamine-deficiency mechanism proposed is explicitly speculative in the sources reviewed.
Evidence: WEAK/PRELIMINARY as a formal diagnostic construct — RSD is **not in the DSM-5** and has limited controlled research; treat the label as practitioner shorthand for a real and clinically observed phenomenon (heightened emotional reactivity to perceived rejection is better supported broadly in ADHD-adjacent emotional dysregulation research) rather than as an established, separately-measurable syndrome.
Sources: [Cleveland Clinic — Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/24099-rejection-sensitive-dysphoria-rsd), [ADDA — Rejection Sensitivity](https://add.org/rejection-sensitivity/)

**F17. The "Wall of Awful" (Brendan Mahan) is a coaching metaphor, not a research construct, but it names a real and design-relevant phenomenon: accumulated negative-association "bricks" around a task category that raise the activation cost of starting, independent of the task's actual difficulty.**
Mahan (ADHD coach, not a researcher) describes each past bad experience with a task-type (a missed deadline, a stinging comment) as a "brick" that makes future initiation harder, disproportionate to task complexity. Commonly cited examples: email, multi-step projects, reading, writing — all directly relevant to Riemann's target content types.
Evidence: WEAK/PRELIMINARY (practitioner/coaching theory, no controlled measurement found) — but flagged explicitly as lived-experience/practitioner knowledge per the brief, and worth taking seriously as a design constraint even without RCT backing.
Sources: [Life Skills Advocate — Why Does The Wall Of Awful Make Easy Tasks Feel Impossible](https://lifeskillsadvocate.com/blog/wall-of-awful/), [Psychology Today — Getting Started on Inexplicably Tough Tasks](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/empowerment-is-real/202508/getting-started-on-inexplicably-tough-tasks)

**F18. Body doubling is enormously popular (Focusmate, Flow Club, Flown) but has almost no controlled evidence.**
A 2026 arXiv paper on VR body doubling explicitly states controlled studies have yet to show conclusive effects and the mechanisms remain unclear; its own pilot (n=12) found faster task completion and perceived accuracy/attention with a human or AI body double vs. working alone — suggestive but far too small to generalize.
Evidence: WEAK/PRELIMINARY. Explicitly do not treat as validated.
Source: [You Are Not Alone: Designing Body Doubling for ADHD in Virtual Reality — arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153)

### Medication and state

**F19. Stimulant medication produces large, time-bounded windows of altered cognitive state, which is a first-class adaptivity signal even without any biometric input.**
Short-acting stimulants: onset 30–45 min, wear off in 3–6 hours. Long-acting/extended-release: symptom control 8–12 hours, with lisdexamfetamine showing efficacy out to ~14 hours post-dose. RTV specifically (F2) is reduced by stimulants (g ≈ -0.74) but not by non-stimulant treatment. Notably, one source states stimulants improve attention/concentration but do **not** reliably improve learning/academic achievement in adult college students with ADHD — i.e., medication changes the attentional substrate but doesn't necessarily change comprehension/retention outcomes on its own.
Evidence: MODERATE-STRONG for onset/duration pharmacokinetics (well-established prescribing information); MODERATE for the "doesn't improve academic learning" claim (fewer, more contested studies; flagged in secondary source, primary study not independently verified here).
Sources: [Duration of effect of oral long-acting stimulant medications — PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20491612/), [Cognitive effects of stimulant medications, adults with ADHD — ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0149763410000801)

## Design implications for Riemann

1. **Never assume a flat attention budget across a session.** Given RTV (F2) and delay aversion (F3), the zoom dial should let a user re-enter a document at *any* level at any time without penalty — no "you have to start from L0" flow, because the moment they can engage (post-lapse) is unpredictable, not schedulable.
2. **Front-load a payoff before the deep dive.** Delay aversion (F3) means the L0 gist isn't just a convenience, it's an anti-avoidance mechanism — it should render near-instantly (sub-second perceived latency) so the "wait cost" of even opening a large document is close to zero. This is the single highest-leverage design lever from the delay-aversion literature.
3. **Treat L0/L1 generation as the Wall-of-Awful bypass.** Per F17, the felt difficulty of *starting* with a raw document is disproportionate to its true complexity. The abstraction tree's real product value may be less "compression for speed" and more "an entry point with zero accumulated bad-association bricks" — frame L0 explicitly as a low-stakes preview, not a commitment to read the whole thing.
4. **Offer synced text+audio (bimodal) at L2/L3, not just TTS-as-alternative.** F7 shows real benefit from simultaneous, not sequential, audio+text — implement highlight-as-you-listen (word or sentence-level sync) rather than a plain "read aloud" toggle, and prioritize it for longer/denser levels where reading alone is more taxing.
5. **Do not build bionic-reading-style bolded-prefix rendering as a serious feature; if included at all, label it as unproven/opt-in only.** F8 is close to a debunk. Spend that engineering budget on bimodal audio sync instead.
6. **Do not silently pipe in background noise as a default "focus mode."** F9's subtype-reversal finding means a naive "ambient white noise" feature could measurably hurt a meaningful fraction of users. If offered, make it opt-in, per-session togglable, and ideally let the user learn from their own usage whether it helps them (see Signals below) rather than assuming benefit.
7. **Don't build any "hold still" or strict focus-lock UI chrome.** F10 argues fidget-friendly, movement-tolerant interaction (e.g., not penalizing scrolling/dial-jiggling, not auto-pausing on small mouse movements) is more evidence-aligned than a rigid "stay on this passage" enforcement.
8. **Follow W3C COGA patterns directly for the page chrome** (F11): short chunks with generous white space, plain short sentences at L0/L1, consistent/familiar navigation across sessions (the dial position and controls shouldn't move), an always-available "help/get unstuck" affordance, and an explicit way to turn off any animation/distraction.
9. **Consider a lightweight "time made visible" element**, borrowing from Tiimo's success (F15): show elapsed/remaining read-time estimate live as the user scrolls or the dial moves, directly countering time blindness (F6) during a reading session — this is cheap to build (word-count-based) and addresses a well-evidenced deficit.
10. **Immediate, low-stakes feedback on interaction, not gamification-for-its-own-sake.** F13's RCT evidence for gamification is pediatric and about learning tasks, not reading; the safer generalization is "make every dial movement and level-change feel instantly, visibly responsive" (smooth animated transitions, live word-count/read-time updates) rather than adding points/streaks, which risk feeling patronizing to an adult engineering student and aren't evidenced for this specific use case.
11. **Medication/time-of-day state (F19) is a strong argument for a manual, cheap "how much capacity do I have right now" input** (already in Riemann's stated principles) rather than trying to infer it — the pharmacokinetic windows are large, individual, and not observable from interaction alone.

## Signals Riemann could measure

- **Dial dwell time and reversal count per level** — frequent back-and-forth between L0/L1 and L2/L3 without settling could indicate low working-memory capacity in the moment (F1) or RTV-driven attentional drift (F2); a low-load view could auto-suggest itself.
- **Time-to-first-interaction after opening a document** — a long stall before any dial movement is a plausible low-friction proxy for Wall-of-Awful avoidance (F17); could trigger surfacing the L0 gist more prominently, or a gentle "just the one-liner" nudge.
- **Abandonment level** (the depth at which a user stops engaging with a given document) — clustering documents by abandonment depth over time could reveal per-user or per-topic Wall-of-Awful patterns without ever asking the user directly.
- **Session-relative read-time-estimate accuracy** — if Riemann shows an estimated read time and later logs actual time spent, systematic over/under-runs are a passive time-blindness signal (F6) that could calibrate future estimates per-user.
- **Self-reported vs. behavioral energy mismatch** — if Sam sets "high capacity" but dial behavior looks low-capacity (frequent reversals, long stalls), that discrepancy itself is informative and worth surfacing gently rather than silently overriding his stated state.
- **Opt-in noise/ambient-audio toggle usage correlated with subsequent dwell/reversal metrics** — since F9 shows the effect is subtype-dependent and can reverse, Riemann is well placed to let each user's own data answer "does this help me" rather than relying on population-level findings.

## Contradictions and caveats

- **Mind-wandering and DMN**: the popular "DMN doesn't switch off in ADHD, causing mind-wandering" story (repeated across many secondary sources) is *not* what the more careful adolescent study found — that study tied DMN connectivity to delay aversion/temporal discounting specifically, **not** to mind-wandering (F5). Any Riemann copy or design rationale citing "DMN causes distraction" should be softened.
- **White noise**: directly contradicts a simple "add ambient sound to help focus" recommendation — the same intervention helps roughly two-thirds and hurts roughly a third of an ADHD sample, split along inattentive vs. hyperactive/impulsive lines (F9). Population-level advice doesn't transfer safely to one user (Sam) without knowing which profile he has, and even then it's a single, unreplicated subgroup finding.
- **Bimodal reading vs. cognitive load theory**: F7's benefit for bimodal reading sits in some tension with the fleet's baseline Cognitive Load Theory (split-attention effect, redundancy effect) — simultaneous audio+text is technically redundant dual-channel input, which CLT would predict *could* increase extraneous load. The resolution in the literature seems to be that it helps *weaker* readers and vocabulary acquisition specifically, and the benefit is less clear or reverses for skilled/fluent readers — this is a genuine, unresolved-in-the-literature tension worth testing empirically in Riemann itself rather than assuming.
- **Gamification evidence is pediatric**; extrapolating an 8-week children's RCT (F13) to an adult engineering student reading dense technical/academic material is a stretch. Treat gamification recommendations as directional, not evidence-backed for this specific population.
- **RSD and Wall of Awful are not clinical diagnoses** — useful language for design intuition, actively used in the ADHD coaching/lived-experience community, but should not be presented to Sam or in documentation as established science. The brief's evidence-tagging discipline matters especially here since these concepts are extremely popular in ADHD media relative to their actual evidence base.
- **Adult vs. child evidence base generally**: a large fraction of the strongest ADHD cognitive-science findings (RTV, delay aversion, DMN) are either child-derived or mixed-age; the adult-specific effect sizes tend to be smaller (F2's g=0.46 adults vs 0.76 children) — don't assume child-derived magnitudes transfer directly to Sam.
- **Single-subject generalization**: essentially everything above is group-level evidence; Sam's individual profile (which ADHD presentation, medication status, engineering-domain reading habits) could deviate from any given average. This argues structurally for the "measure signals, let the system adapt per-user" approach over "bake in population-level defaults and don't revisit them."

## Gaps (for the next wave)

1. **ADHD-specific research on hierarchical/multi-level summarization UIs specifically** (as opposed to video customization or body-doubling) is thin — nobody I found has studied a "zoom dial across abstraction levels" pattern directly for ADHD readers. This is genuinely novel territory; worth a targeted search of UIST/IUI/UMAP (not just CHI/ASSETS) and of semantic-zoom / progressive-disclosure HCI literature intersected with ADHD or with cognitive-accessibility populations more broadly (claude-spatial-researcher and claude-abstraction-researcher's areas overlap here — worth cross-checking with them).
2. **Reading speed and comprehension trade-offs specific to adult ADHD** (not children, not dyslexia) were surprisingly hard to find as a clean, separate literature — most reading-intervention research is either general or dyslexia-focused with ADHD as a comorbid mention. A dedicated search for "adult ADHD reading rate" / "ADHD reading comprehension eye-tracking" could sharpen the L1 word-count/pacing assumptions in the plan.
