# 08 — Fluctuating attention, motivation and energy: state-adaptive design

## TL;DR
- ADHD's core cognitive signature is *within-person* inconsistency, not just low average performance: reaction-time variability is one of the most replicated ADHD markers (meta-analysis of 319 studies). Riemann should design for a moving baseline, not a fixed "user profile."
- Time-of-day matters, but the textbook "synchrony effect" (perform best at your chronotype's optimal time) is weaker and less general than commonly claimed — real but inconsistent, task-dependent, and confounded with practice/motivation.
- Stimulant medication (if used) creates a predictable multi-hour capacity curve (onset ~20–30 min, peak ~1–3 h, wear-off/crash at 3–4 h for IR, longer for XR) — this is a legitimate, learnable input signal, not just "time of day."
- Motivation is not a single dial. SDT (autonomy/competence/relatedness), curiosity/information-gap theory, and interest development (Hidi & Renninger) each suggest different levers Riemann can pull — and they're pullable independent of raw energy level.
- Sleep loss and anxiety degrade different things (sleep hits sustained attention/working memory broadly; anxiety specifically hijacks the central executive's inhibition/shifting functions) — so "low capacity" and "anxious" should be different states with different UI responses, not one "bad day" flag.
- Interruption/resumption research (Altmann & Trafton) gives a concrete mechanism for "where was I" cues: a short interruption *lag* with a chance to rehearse the goal before interruption sharply cuts resumption cost — this is buildable.
- The Ovsiankina effect (pull to resume unfinished tasks) replicates much better than the classic Zeigarnik memory-enhancement claim — treat "unfinished work nags at you" as real, but don't build features that assume interruption improves recall.
- Streak mechanics are actively hostile to ADHD's variable-consistency profile (asymmetric loss framing, all-or-nothing reset) — this is a documented complaint pattern, not just intuition, though the specific evidence is thinner (practitioner/blog-level) than the cognitive-science findings above.
- Implementation intentions ("if X then I'll do Y") have one of the more robust effect sizes in behavior-change research (d≈.65, meta-analysis of 94 tests, N>8,000) — cheap to implement as a prompt pattern.
- JITAI/micro-randomized-trial methodology (Nahum-Shani et al.) is the right frame for *how* to adapt Riemann over time: don't hand-tune rules, treat "when to intervene" as something to learn from lightweight, repeated, low-stakes randomization.

## Findings

### Within-person variability is the core phenomenon, not a side effect
**Claim:** People with ADHD show markedly higher intra-individual (moment-to-moment) variability in reaction time and performance than neurotypical peers, across hundreds of studies, and this variability is considered by some researchers more diagnostic than mean-level slowness.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (meta-analysis of 319 studies).
**Sources:** [Reaction time variability in ADHD: A meta-analytic review of 319 studies (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272735813000871), [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23872284/), [Kofler et al. PDF, FSU](https://psy.fsu.edu/clc/Publications/CLC_Kofler2013.pdf) (fetch of the PDF itself failed — binary; relying on search-result abstract and PubMed listing, flagged below).
**Caveat:** I could not get clean full-text access to pull the exact effect size (d) from this meta-analysis — the PDF returned as unreadable binary and PubMed served a bot-check page instead of the abstract. The finding (higher variability, robust across studies) is corroborated by multiple independent secondary sources but I did not verify the precise pooled effect size myself. Treat the direction as STRONG, the specific magnitude as unverified.

### EMA studies confirm ADHD affect/attention fluctuates within the day, and can be measured with acceptable burden
**Claim:** Ecological momentary assessment (repeated smartphone-based in-the-moment surveys) is feasible in ADHD populations, with compliance rates of 60–99% in one systematic review, and captures meaningful within-day variability in emotion dysregulation and functional impairment that retrospective (end-of-day/week) surveys miss.
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE (systematic review + several small feasibility studies; ADHD EMA literature is still young and samples are often small/adolescent).
**Sources:** [EMA of Emotion Dysregulation in ADHD: A Systematic Review (Psychiatric Quarterly)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11126-026-10291-6), [Feasibility of EMA of Negative Emotion in Girls with ADHD (pilot)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033294119838757), [Naturalistic study of functional impairment, early morning vs late afternoon/evening (clinicaltrials.gov protocol)](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04473755), [Illuminating daily life experiences of adolescents with/without ADHD (EMA protocol)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10546102/).
**Caveat:** Most of this literature is in children/adolescents and skews toward emotion dysregulation rather than attention/reading comprehension specifically. Generalizing to a single adult engineering student doing knowledge work is a real transfer gap.

### The chronotype "synchrony effect" is real in aggregate but weaker/less general than folk wisdom claims
**Claim:** People tend to perform slightly better on some cognitive tasks at their chronotype-congruent time of day (morning types in the morning, evening types in the evening/afternoon), most clearly for fluid-intelligence-type tasks; but a large fraction of studies (reportedly >80% in one review) find no significant chronotype main effect, and effects are task-dependent.
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE, leaning WEAK for a strong universal effect — the review itself frames this as evidence being "rather mixed" despite common acceptance.
**Sources:** [Chronotype and synchrony effects in human cognitive performance: A systematic review, Chronobiology International 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07420528.2025.2490495) (fetch blocked by 403 — relying on search-engine summary, not full text, flagged), [The synchrony effect revisited (Chronobiology Int'l 2018)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/07420528.2018.1500477), [The Interplay of Time-of-day and Chronotype Results in No General and Robust Cognitive Boost (Collabra: Psychology)](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/9/1/88337/197502/The-Interplay-of-Time-of-day-and-Chronotype).
**Caveat:** I was blocked (403) from the full 2025 systematic review and am relying on the search snippet. The title of one directly-relevant paper ("...Results in No General and Robust Cognitive Boost") is itself a useful corrective to over-designing around chronotype.

### Post-lunch dip and ultradian rhythm are real, circadian-driven, and largely involuntary
**Claim:** The early-afternoon alertness dip is a genuine circadian phenomenon (not just "ate a big lunch") — it occurs even without lunch and even when the person doesn't know the time. It reflects a ~12-hour harmonic ("circasemidian") riding on the ~24h core-temperature/alertness curve, with EEG signatures (increased theta/alpha) of drowsiness. Shorter (~90 min) ultradian/BRAC cycles are more pronounced during circadian troughs.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG for the phenomenon's existence and circadian basis; MODERATE for the ultradian/BRAC framing as something with reliable minute-scale predictive value for an individual.
**Sources:** [Circadian determinants of the postlunch dip in performance (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8877121/), [Modeling Napping, Post-Lunch Dip, and Other Variations in Human Sleep Propensity (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2647793/), [The Post-Lunch Dip in Performance (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7848298_The_Post-Lunch_Dip_in_Performance), [Circasemidian rhythm (Wikipedia — secondary, used for terminology orientation only)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circasemidian_rhythm).

### Sleep deprivation broadly impairs attention and working memory, with partial compensation
**Claim:** Both total and partial sleep deprivation reduce sustained/selective attention and working memory performance (increased RT, reduced accuracy), mediated by altered connectivity between attention, default-mode, and fronto-parietal brain networks. Some compensatory mechanisms partially offset working-memory decline but don't eliminate it.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (large, long-standing literature; multiple converging reviews).
**Sources:** [Sleep deprivation effects on basic cognitive processes (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8340886/), [Effects of Sleep Deprivation on Working Memory: functional connectivity (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7588803/), [Sleep deprivation: Impact on cognitive performance (Taylor & Francis)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.2147/ndt.s12160203).

### Stimulant medication has a well-characterized time-course that functions as a predictable capacity signal
**Claim:** Immediate-release methylphenidate: onset 20–30 min, peak effect 1–3 h, wears off ~3–4 h (half-life 2–4 h) — explaining the classic multi-dose pattern and mid-afternoon "crash." Extended-release formulations show a biphasic profile (two peaks, e.g. ~2 h and ~6 h post-dose) and last 8–12 h.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (pharmacokinetic data, though the summary sources are practitioner/clinical rather than a peer-reviewed head-to-head).
**Sources:** [Time Course of Multilayer-Release Methylphenidate (Wigal et al., J Attention Disorders)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054716672335), [Methylphenidate mechanism/dosing overview](https://psychscenehub.com/psychbytes/methylphenidate-mechanism-of-action-side-effects-and-dosing/), [Efficacy and Duration of Action of ADHD Medications](https://www.adxs.org/en/page/434/efficacy-and-duration-of-action-of-adhd-medications).
**Caveat:** This is population-level PK data; the actual subjective "wear-off" experience (irritability, rebound inattention) varies a lot person to person and dose to dose. Should be a signal Riemann *lets the user declare* (self-report timing), not one it infers from indirect behavior with confidence.

### Self-Determination Theory: three needs, not one motivation dial
**Claim:** Autonomy, competence, and relatedness are theorized (and broadly supported across decades of SDT research) as separate psychological needs; satisfying them predicts intrinsic motivation and well-being, and thwarting any one degrades motivation even if the others are fine.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG as a broad framework (large, long-running research program), though — as with most broad psych frameworks — individual mechanistic claims vary in strength.
**Sources:** [Self-determination theory: A quarter century of human motivation research (APA)](https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory), [Ryan & Deci 2000 original SDT paper (PDF)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf).
**Design relevance:** A "gist first" abstraction tree is inherently competence-supportive (fast wins, visible progress) and can be autonomy-supportive (user picks zoom level) — but Riemann currently has no relatedness lever at all, and no explicit low-friction way to expose "why this matters to you" (value), which the next finding covers.

### Temporal Motivation Theory / procrastination equation: motivation degrades with delay and impulsivity, not just low expectancy/value
**Claim:** Steel & König's TMT formalizes motivation as (Expectancy × Value) / (1 + Impulsiveness × Delay). Steel's 2007 meta-analysis (691 correlations, 216 studies) supports the general predictive pattern (~r=0.45 for the composite model's relationship to procrastination).
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE-STRONG (large meta-analysis behind the underlying correlational claims; the *specific equation form* is more a useful organizing heuristic than a precisely validated formula).
**Sources:** [Temporal Motivation Theory: Formula or Folly? (Psychology Today — critical take)](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/dont-delay/200803/temporal-motivation-theory-formula-or-folly), [Temporal Motivation Theory overview](https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/temporal-motivation-theory-steel-konig-procrastination/), [Wikipedia: Procrastination (secondary, used for the meta-analysis figure)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procrastination).
**Caveat:** I flag the "Formula or Folly?" piece myself — even sympathetic reviewers note the equation is more a mnemonic/organizing device than a literally validated quantitative model. Treat "shrink delay, raise vividness of value, reduce impulsiveness-triggering friction" as the actionable takeaway, not the arithmetic.
**Design relevance:** This directly motivates the zoom dial: reducing *delay* to a legible payoff (an L0 gist available in seconds) is a direct lever on the denominator of the equation, independent of the content's intrinsic value.

### Curiosity/information-gap theory: curiosity is triggered by a *known, bounded* gap, and it enhances memory broadly
**Claim:** Loewenstein's information-gap theory: curiosity arises when people perceive a gap between what they know and what they want to know, particularly when the answer feels knowable/attainable. Gruber et al. (2014, Neuron) found curious states activate dopaminergic reward circuitry (substantia nigra/VTA, nucleus accumbens) and enhance hippocampal memory not just for the curiosity-triggering content but for *incidental* information encountered during the curious state.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG for the Gruber 2014 finding (widely cited, mechanistic neuroimaging study); MODERATE for information-gap theory as a fully general account of curiosity (it's one of several competing/complementary curiosity theories).
**Sources:** [Curiosity, Information Gaps, and the Utility of Knowledge (Golman & Loewenstein, CMU)](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/golman_loewenstein_curiosity.pdf), [The Psychology and Neuroscience of Curiosity (Gruber & Ranganath review, ScienceDirect/Neuron)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627315007679).
**Design relevance:** L0/L1 gists that pose an explicit, bounded question ("the gap") rather than just stating a compressed fact may recruit more curiosity-driven engagement and better incidental retention of surrounding detail — worth testing as an L0 phrasing style ("the surprising reason X happened" vs "X happened because...").

### Interest development is phased — situational interest needs different support than mature individual interest
**Claim:** Hidi & Renninger's four-phase model: triggered situational interest → maintained situational interest → emerging individual interest → well-developed individual interest, each with different affect/knowledge/value profiles.
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE-STRONG as the dominant framework in educational psychology for interest (widely cited, but the model itself is more descriptive/organizing than causally tested end-to-end).
**Sources:** [The Four-Phase Model of Interest Development (Hidi & Renninger, Educational Psychologist 2006 — accessed via Semantic Scholar/ERIC listings)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ736298), [Swarthmore repository copy](https://works.swarthmore.edu/fac-education/12/).
**Design relevance:** For topics the user has no prior interest in (cold document, e.g. a random PDF someone sent), Riemann should behave differently than for a topic in the user's declared domain (e.g. robotics for the ECE4078 project) — early-phase interest needs external triggers/novelty and low-stakes engagement, not depth-first drilling.

### Flow requires clear goals, immediate feedback, and challenge-skill balance — and is legible enough to detect
**Claim:** Csikszentmihalyi's flow model: matched challenge/skill, clear proximal goals, and immediate feedback are necessary conditions; hallmark subjective signs include distorted time perception and loss of self-consciousness.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG as a descriptive framework (one of the most replicated constructs in positive psychology), though flow's *causal* structure (which condition is necessary vs. merely correlated) is debated in the methodological literature.
**Sources:** [Investigating the "Flow" Experience: Key Conceptual and Operational Issues (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7033418/), [Flow State in Learning: Theory and Classroom Strategies](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/flow-state).
**Design relevance:** the zoom dial itself can be a flow-supporting mechanic (immediate feedback: content visibly changes as you turn the dial; clear proximal goal: reach the depth you chose) — but only if there's no lag/spinner between turning the dial and seeing the result.

### JITAI / micro-randomized trials: the right paradigm for *learning* when to adapt, not just building fixed rules
**Claim:** JITAIs deliver intervention options tailored to a person's changing internal/contextual state, timed to moments of receptivity. Micro-randomized trials (Nahum-Shani, Murphy et al.) repeatedly randomize the same person to intervention options (potentially many times/day) to causally estimate what works, when, and for whom — this is the standard method for building/validating JITAI decision rules.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG as an established, actively-developed methodology (NIH-funded center, multiple deployed trials e.g. smoking cessation, activity).
**Sources:** [Just-in-Time Adaptive Interventions: Where Are We Now and What Is Next? (Annual Review of Psychology)](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-121024-044244), [Just-in-Time Adaptive Interventions and Adaptive Interventions overview (Susan Murphy lab)](http://people.seas.harvard.edu/~samurphy/JITAI_MRT/jitai.html), [d3center — Advancing mobile health interventions](https://d3c.isr.umich.edu/mobilehealth/), [Microrandomized Trials: Developing JITAIs for Better Public Health (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36413704/).
**Caveat:** This literature is built around clinical/public-health interventions with research infrastructure (formal RCTs, large N). A single-user assistant can borrow the *concept* (log context + outcome, occasionally vary the default, see what correlates with engagement) but can't run a literal adequately-powered MRT on one person. Treat as inspiration for a lightweight "try a variant occasionally and note what happened" pattern, not literal methodology to implement.

### Receptivity and burden are the two things that make or break momentary check-ins
**Claim:** Single-item EMA measures (e.g., one 0–10 slider for fatigue) are a standard, validated way to reduce respondent burden while preserving signal; burden (item count, frequency) trades off directly against compliance and data quality.
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE-STRONG (well-established methodological finding across EMA literature broadly, if not ADHD-specific).
**Sources:** [Use of a Single-Item EMA to Measure Daily Exercise (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10857316), [Balancing burden and resolution: EMA survey schedule effects on compliance (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42330698/), [Participant Compliance with EMA in Movement Behavior Research: Systematic Review (JMIR mHealth)](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2025/1/e52887).
**Design relevance:** if Riemann ever asks a direct energy/mood question, it should be exactly one tap/slider, optional, and never blocking — consistent with the brief's "only asks for what they have capacity to give" principle.

### Passive sensing can infer some behavioral/affective state from phone/computer usage, but not cognitive state directly, and accuracy is limited
**Claim:** Passive smartphone signals (screen checks, typing dynamics, app-switching, battery/dark-mode viewing) have been used to predict impulsivity, anxiety, stress, and some EMA-measured affect states with above-chance accuracy — but current consensus is that sensors cannot directly capture mood/cognitive state; they infer correlates.
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE (multiple small studies, consistent limitation noted across the field) leaning WEAK for reliable, generalizable, real-time inference in a single user without training data.
**Sources:** [mPulse Mobile Sensing Model for Passive Detection of Impulsive Behavior (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7875694/), [Predicting anxiety state using smartphone-based passive sensing (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1532046419300693), [Towards Deep Learning Models for Psychological State Prediction using Smartphone Data (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06350).
**Design relevance:** typing cadence, edit/backspace rate, scroll behavior, and dial-fiddling *within Riemann itself* are a much more direct and privacy-contained signal than phone-wide sensing, and match the brief's "no real-world actions" / low-friction principle better than a general sensing stack would.

### Interruption resumption: a short "lag" with time to encode the goal sharply reduces resumption cost
**Claim:** Altmann & Trafton's goal-activation model and empirical interruption studies show: when there's a brief interruption *lag* (an alert before the interruption proper) during which a person can prospectively encode/rehearse their current goal, they resume the primary task faster than with no lag. Longer/more demanding interruptions increase resumption time; goal rehearsal mitigates memory decay of the interrupted goal.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (converging cognitive-psychology and applied HCI/CogSci studies, replicated across several paradigms).
**Sources:** [Task Interruption: Resumption Lag and the Role of Cues (Altmann, CogSci04 PDF)](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Altmann-CogSci04.pdf), [Preparing to resume an interrupted task (Trafton et al., ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581903000235), [The effect of interruption duration and demand on resuming suspended goals (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19102614/), [Greg Trafton's interruptions research page](https://gregtrafton.com/interruptions/).
**Design relevance:** this is directly buildable — a lightweight, explicit "leaving now" action (even just closing the tab deliberately vs. an ADHD-typical abrupt task-switch) that captures a one-line goal/state snapshot ("what were you trying to get from this") gives Riemann exactly the "prospective encoding" moment the research says helps resumption. This is a much stronger justification for a "bookmark your state" feature than intuition alone.

### Zeigarnik effect (memory boost for unfinished tasks) is weak/non-general; Ovsiankina effect (pull to resume) replicates
**Claim:** A 2025 meta-analysis found no reliable memory advantage for unfinished vs. completed tasks (the classic "Zeigarnik effect" on recall), but did find a general, more robust tendency to spontaneously want to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina effect).
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE (one recent meta-analysis; I could not get full-text access due to a login redirect, so I'm relying on search-engine-summarized abstract-level claims, not the paper itself).
**Sources:** [Interruption, recall and resumption: a meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects (Humanities and Social Sciences Communications / Nature, 2025) — blocked by login wall, relying on search summary](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w), [secondary summary: Zeigarnik and von Restorff (Memory & Cognition)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-020-01033-5).
**Caveat:** explicitly flagging that I did not read this meta-analysis's actual text — access was blocked by an identity-provider redirect. The finding pattern (resumption pull real, memory-enhancement claim shaky) is consistent with what secondary sources and general skepticism about "Zeigarnik effect" pop-psychology claims report, but should be treated as PRELIMINARY pending primary-source confirmation.
**Design relevance:** don't build a feature that assumes "leaving something unfinished helps you remember it later" (weak evidence) — but *do* build on "you'll feel pulled to come back to it" (better evidence), e.g. a lightweight unfinished-reading tray that's visible but not urgent.

### Streaks are widely reported to be a bad fit for ADHD's inconsistency, though the evidence here is largely practitioner/anecdotal, not experimental
**Claim:** Streak mechanics are structurally loss-averse (total reset on one miss vs. proportional loss) and this specifically clashes with ADHD's day-to-day inconsistency, producing shame spirals and app abandonment; some ADHD users reportedly deliberately break long streaks to relieve pressure.
**Evidence strength:** WEAK/PRELIMINARY as *ADHD-specific causal evidence* — sources found are health-content marketing blogs and app-review aggregation, not peer-reviewed studies. The *general* loss-aversion mechanism (losses ~2x as motivating as equivalent gains) is well-established in behavioral economics, but its specific application to ADHD streak-breaking is not independently verified here.
**Sources:** [Breaking The Chain: Why Streak Features Fail ADHD Users (Klarity Health — practitioner/marketing blog, flagged as pop-science tier)](https://www.helloklarity.com/post/breaking-the-chain-why-streak-features-fail-adhd-users-and-how-to-design-better-alternatives/), [I Tested 10 Habit Trackers in 30 Days (Medium, personal account)](https://medium.com/@wardtylerd/i-tested-10-habit-trackers-in-30-days-8-broke-me-the-same-way-9803ea20b228).
**Design relevance:** given the brief already states "no streak punishment" as a design principle, this finding *supports* that principle but shouldn't be over-cited as if it were peer-reviewed. Frame it in the design doc as "consistent with loss-aversion theory and widely reported ADHD user complaints" rather than "proven."

### Implementation intentions are one of the higher-effect-size, cheap-to-implement behavior-change tools available
**Claim:** Gollwitzer & Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis (94 independent tests, >8,000 participants) found implementation intentions ("if situation Y, then I will do X") have a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d≈.65), working by increasing accessibility of the trigger situation and automating the response.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (large, well-cited meta-analysis; effect size is one of the more robust in the behavior-change literature).
**Sources:** [Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement: A Meta-Analysis (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/37367696_Implementation_Intentions_and_Goal_Achievement_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Effects_and_Processes), [Gollwitzer, Implementation Intentions (NIH/cancercontrol.gov PDF)](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal_intent_attain.pdf).
**Design relevance:** a low-friction "if I open this on my phone, show L0 only" or "if it's after 9pm, default to L1 not L3" is exactly the implementation-intention pattern, and could be offered as a one-time setup choice rather than inferred.

### Habits are driven by stable context cues, largely independent of momentary goals or motivation
**Claim:** Wendy Wood's research: ~43% of daily behavior is habitual; habits are activated by recurring context cues (place, preceding action) and are relatively insensitive to short-term goal changes — meaning consistency of *context*, not willpower, is what builds automaticity, and moving to a new context breaks habitual autopilot and reinstates goal-driven behavior.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (well-established program of research, multiple converging studies).
**Sources:** [A New Look at Habits and the Habit–Goal Interface (Wood & Neal, Psychological Review, PDF)](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/wp-content/uploads/sites/183/2023/10/wood.neal_.2007psychrev_a_new_look_at_habits_and_the_interface_between_habits_and_goals.pdf), [PS67CH11-Wood review (Annual Review of Psychology, PDF)](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/wp-content/uploads/sites/183/2023/10/wood.runger.2016.pdf).
**Design relevance:** this cuts against relying on the user's motivation/willpower each session and argues for a highly consistent entry ritual/anchor (matches the spatial-view "one fixed home anchor" principle already in the brief) — the anchor's *consistency*, not its cleverness, is what will eventually make opening Riemann effortless.

### Anxiety specifically degrades the central executive (inhibition, shifting), not general capacity uniformly
**Claim:** Eysenck & Derakshan's Attentional Control Theory: anxiety impairs the goal-directed attentional system and increases stimulus-driven (threat-biased) attention; it particularly hits central-executive functions of inhibition and attentional shifting. Critically, anxiety can preserve *effectiveness* (output quality) at the cost of *efficiency* (more effort/resources spent) — so an anxious user might still produce fine output while burning much more capacity to get there.
**Evidence strength:** STRONG (foundational, heavily cited theory with substantial supporting/refining literature).
**Sources:** [Anxiety and Cognitive Performance: Attentional Control Theory (Eysenck et al. 2007, PDF)](https://tu-dresden.de/die_tu_dresden/fakultaeten/fakultaet_mathematik_und_naturwissenschaften/fachrichtung_psychologie/i1/allgpsy/lehre/pruefungsliteratur_KN_2013/Eysenck-2007.pdf), [Anxiety, Processing Efficiency, and Cognitive Performance: New Developments (Hogrefe)](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/abs/10.1027/1016-9040.14.2.168).
**Design relevance:** this is the clearest evidence for the brief's assumption that load capacity and emotional state are *separate* signals — a user can be anxious but still "capable" in a narrow sense (they'll push through), which argues against inferring emotional state purely from performance/output quality. It also argues for reducing switching/inhibition demands specifically (fewer competing choices, clearer single next action) when anxiety is flagged, distinct from just "simplify because tired."

### Self-compassion correlates with better coping, less procrastination, and less self-handicapping around failure/incompletion
**Claim:** Higher self-compassion is associated with better coping with academic setbacks, greater enjoyment of learning, mastery (vs. performance) goal orientation, and fewer self-handicapping behaviors like procrastination; it's linked to lower fear of failure.
**Evidence strength:** MODERATE-STRONG (substantial correlational literature from Kristin Neff's research program; mostly correlational rather than causal/experimental in the sources found).
**Sources:** [Self-Compassion: Theory, Method, Research (Neff, Annual Review of Psychology 2023, PDF)](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Neff-2023.pdf), [Self-Compassion, Self-Esteem, and Well-Being (Neff, PDF)](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/SC.SE_.Well-being.pdf).
**Design relevance:** relevant to *tone*, not just mechanics — when Riemann detects a likely low-capacity or anxious state, self-compassion research argues for non-judgmental framing ("here's the short version for now" rather than "you didn't finish this") over neutral silence, since shame/self-criticism specifically predict avoidance.

## Design implications for Riemann

1. **Two independently-adjustable state dimensions, matching the brief's own framing, now with mechanism.** Capacity (energy/attention bandwidth) and affect (anxiety/mood) should drive *different* UI responses because they impair different things (ACT: anxiety → executive/inhibition; sleep/fatigue → broad attention/WM). A "low capacity, calm" state should look different from a "moderate capacity, anxious" state — the former wants less content and less choice; the latter wants less *switching* and more grounding, even if it can tolerate normal content volume.

2. **Default zoom level should be a function of declared/inferred capacity, with L0 as the universal safe floor.** Given RT-variability and EMA evidence that ADHD attention is unpredictable session-to-session, Riemann should never assume "yesterday's good session predicts today." Every open should start at L0 (or last-used level, user's choice) rather than assuming continuity — cheap insurance against the intra-individual variability finding.

3. **Build a genuine "state bookmark," justified by the Altmann & Trafton interruption-lag finding.** On closing/switching away, offer (never force) a one-tap capture of "where you were" — ideally auto-suggested from the anchored node (`data-node-id` the dial was centered on) plus one optional free-text word. This is the *prospective encoding* moment the research says cuts resumption cost — it should feel like "leaving a note for future-you," not a save-progress mechanic.

4. **No streaks; if any continuity mechanic exists, make it non-resettable and proportional.** E.g., a rolling 7-day "engaged days" count that just doesn't move on a missed day, rather than a chain that breaks. This matches the brief's forgiving-design instinct and the (weak-evidence-but-plausible) streak-shame pattern, while not over-relying on the thin sourcing there.

5. **Use implementation-intention phrasing for any settings/defaults, not just nudges in the moment.** Instead of inferring "it's evening, show less," let the user set (once, at low cost) "if it's after 9pm, open at L1" — this borrows the d≈.65 effect directly rather than betting on real-time inference accuracy, which the passive-sensing literature says is currently weak.

6. **L0 gists can be phrased as bounded questions/gaps, not just compressed statements, as an opt-in style.** Given Gruber 2014's finding that curiosity-state exposure improves incidental memory broadly, an L0 that poses "the gap" ("what actually caused X") rather than flatly stating the answer may pull more of the reader in and improve retention of L1/L2 content encountered right after — worth an A/B-style test (see JITAI note below) rather than assuming.

7. **Treat hyperfocus as a state to support, not fight — but add a lightweight, non-intrusive overrun guard.** For high-capacity/absorbed states, Riemann should not throttle depth (let L3 stay available, don't nag about zoom level) — but given hyperfocus's known association with missed time/commitments, a *very* unobtrusive elapsed-time indicator (not an alarm) fits: presence without interruption, consistent with "it only asks for what they have capacity to give."

8. **Borrow the JITAI/MRT mindset at small scale: occasionally vary a default and passively note engagement, rather than hand-tuning rules forever.** E.g., alternate between two L0 phrasing styles or two default zoom levels for otherwise-similar sessions and log which one the user drills deeper from — not a formal trial, but the same underlying logic (learn receptivity from lightweight repeated variation instead of guessing once).

9. **Context-anchor the "home" screen physically/visually, not just logically, to build habit automaticity per Wood.** Since habits form around consistent *context* more than motivation, the existing "stable home anchor" principle in the brief should be protected from redesign churn even when new features are added — novelty should attach to peripheral regions, exactly as the baseline research already states, and this finding gives an independent mechanistic reason why (habit-context binding breaks if the anchor itself moves).

### A concrete state model for Riemann

Two independent axes, each with 3 coarse bands (coarse because precision isn't supportable given the evidence above; more bands would overfit noisy signals):

| Axis | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capacity** (energy/attention bandwidth — from sleep, time-of-day, medication phase, fatigue) | can barely engage | normal working state | absorbed/hyperfocus-capable |
| **Affect** (anxious/wired ↔ calm, independent of capacity per Attentional Control Theory) | anxious/threat-primed | neutral | calm/settled |

**How each is inferred** (weighted toward explicit/cheap signals over inferred ones, per the passive-sensing weakness noted above):
- Capacity: optional 1-tap slider at session start (skippable) > self-declared medication timing (if opted in) > implicit dial-hesitation/dwell/abandonment signals accumulated over the session, never a single-session hard read.
- Affect: optional 1-tap separate check (never merged with capacity) > implicit signal only if very high-confidence (e.g., rapid repeated backing-out of content), otherwise default to "unknown/neutral" rather than guess.
- Both default to Medium/neutral with no data — never block or gate the app on missing signal (matches "useful with zero input").

**How each state changes Riemann's defaults:**

| State | Default zoom | Modality | Density | Pacing | Prompts/tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low capacity (any affect) | L0 only, L1 available on request | text-first, no audio demands | one thing on screen at a time | no auto-advance, no timers | none unless asked; self-compassion-consistent tone if any copy appears ("here's the short version" not "you didn't finish") |
| Medium capacity, neutral/calm affect | L1 default, dial fully live | text + optional audio | normal | normal | occasional, skippable ("want the next level?") |
| Medium/High capacity, anxious affect | L1 default but fewer competing choices/tabs open at once (per ACT: protect inhibition/shifting, not volume) | text-first; avoid multi-source comparison views | reduced *choice* density, not necessarily content density | slower forced pacing avoided — don't add friction, remove decision points instead | grounding-first micro-copy optional, never forced |
| High capacity, calm/absorbed (possible hyperfocus) | last-used or L3, dial stays fully open | any | full, including side-by-side/compare views | no throttling; unobtrusive elapsed-time indicator only, no interruption | none — protect the state, don't nag |

This model is deliberately coarse (2 axes × 3 bands = 9 cells, most defaults collapsing to ~4 distinct behaviors) because the evidence above supports "capacity and affect are different and each fluctuates unpredictably," not "we can finely rank today's exact state." Precision should come from the JITAI-lite approach (§ Design implications #8) learned per-user over time, not from a more elaborate model designed up front.

## Signals Riemann could measure

- **Implicit, from within-app interaction (low-friction, no sensing needed):**
  - Dial-fiddling rate / hesitation before choosing a level (possible proxy for indecision or low capacity)
  - Time-to-first-interaction after opening a document (possible proxy for energy/engagement)
  - Depth reached and dwell time per level (L0-only vs. drilling to L3) as an implicit interest/capacity signal
  - Typing/edit cadence and backspace rate in any free-text capture (used cautiously — evidence for inferring cognitive state from this is only MODERATE-WEAK per the passive-sensing findings above)
  - Session length and abandonment point (did they leave mid-L1, mid-L3, or after reaching a natural end)
  - Time since last session and time-of-day of session start, primarily to *ask* the user how it correlates for them, not to silently infer state

- **Explicit, single-tap/low-burden (per the EMA burden findings — never more than one item, always skippable):**
  - A single optional energy/capacity slider or emoji at open, never mandatory, never blocking content
  - A separate, distinct optional affect check (not conflated with capacity) — e.g., "calm / wired / anxious," reflecting the ACT-motivated case for treating these as different variables
  - Self-declared medication timing (if the user opts in) — since PK time-course is a much stronger, cheaper signal than inferred state, and the user already knows it

- **Explicitly out of scope / to avoid:** phone-wide passive sensing (screen-on time, app switching outside Riemann, location, dark-mode viewing) — evidence for its accuracy is weak-to-moderate even in dedicated research studies with training data, and it cuts against the brief's "never takes real-world actions" / minimal-footprint spirit. Any state inference should stay inside what Riemann itself observes.

## Contradictions and caveats

- The chronotype synchrony effect is a good example of something that "everybody knows" but the actual evidence is mixed (>80% of reviewed studies found no chronotype main effect in one review). Don't hard-code strong time-of-day defaults on faith; treat time-of-day as one weak prior among several, and prefer the user's own explicit signal (including self-reported medication timing) over inferred chronotype.
- Two structurally similar findings — Zeigarnik (memory boost from interruption) and Ovsiankina (behavioral pull to resume) — get conflated constantly in pop-science and even in some product-design writing. Only the second replicates well per the 2025 meta-analysis (which I could not fully verify — access blocked). Build resumption features on the *pull-to-resume* claim, not a "leaving it unfinished helps you remember it" claim.
- The ADHD-specific EMA and hyperfocus literature is thin, often small-N, and sometimes non-peer-reviewed adjacent (clinicaltrials.gov protocols describe planned, not completed, studies). Treat any ADHD-specific number (e.g., "68% report hyperfocus") as indicative, not precise.
- The streak/shame claim is the weakest-sourced finding in this report — it rests on marketing-adjacent blog content, not controlled studies, even though it aligns with well-established loss-aversion theory and with the brief's own stated design principle. Flagging explicitly so it isn't mistaken for peer-reviewed evidence in later synthesis.
- Single-user transfer problem, general: almost everything above (EMA, JITAI, MRT, sensing) was validated on populations, often clinical or aggregated across many users. Sam is one adult, with his own idiosyncratic patterns; population findings should seed hypotheses Riemann tests *on him specifically* (the JITAI-lite idea above) rather than be applied as fixed rules.
- I could not access full text for: the Kofler et al. 319-study meta-analysis PDF (returned as unreadable binary), the PubMed abstract page for the same paper (bot-check wall), the 2025 Chronobiology International systematic review (403 blocked), and the 2025 Nature/HSSC Zeigarnik–Ovsiankina meta-analysis (login-wall redirect). Where I've cited these, I'm relying on search-engine-generated summaries of them, not primary-source text I read myself — flagged inline above per finding, and flagged again here for visibility.

## Gaps (for a possible second wave)

- **ADHD + reading/text comprehension specifically**, as opposed to generic RT/attention tasks — almost all the strongest ADHD cognition evidence here is from lab RT tasks, not from reading comprehension or information-seeking tasks, which is Riemann's actual use case. Worth a dedicated search.
- **Digital nudging / notification timing research** (distinct from JITAI's clinical framing) — HCI-specific literature (CHI/UIST) on notification interruption cost and optimal timing in knowledge-work contexts, which would bridge the cognitive-science interruption findings to actual UI patterns.
- **Voice/audio-while-moving and attention** — the brief mentions voice as a background channel and this subtask's brief asked for "audio-while-moving," but I did not find time to dig into dual-task/audio-during-locomotion cognitive load literature. Worth a dedicated pass, possibly overlapping with the modalities worker (04-modalities).
- **Objective, low-burden capacity proxies beyond self-report** — e.g., keystroke dynamics or reading-pace-based cognitive load estimation specifically validated in HCI venues (as opposed to the clinical passive-sensing literature reviewed here), since that's a better-matched literature for an in-app, non-invasive signal.
- **ADHD medication non-use / non-diagnosis population overlap** — this report leans on stimulant PK data assuming medication use; worth explicitly researching non-medicated ADHD day structure and whether/how much of the "predictable capacity curve" logic still applies without a pharmacological anchor.
- **Long-term engagement/retention design specifically for neurodivergent users** (beyond streaks) — e.g., research on novelty-seeking and boredom-proneness in ADHD and how information tools sustain engagement over months, not single sessions.
