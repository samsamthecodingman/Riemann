# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process green plants, algae and some bacteria use to convert light energy into chemical energy. Inside chloroplasts, the pigment chlorophyll absorbs sunlight, mostly in the red and blue parts of the spectrum, and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.

The process has two stages. Light-dependent reactions capture solar energy and split water molecules, releasing oxygen as a byproduct. Light-independent reactions, known as the Calvin cycle, then use that captured energy to fix carbon dioxide from the air into glucose.

This glucose fuels plant growth and, indirectly, almost every food chain on Earth. Because photosynthesis also removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere while releasing oxygen, it plays a central role in regulating the planet's climate and sustaining animal life.
